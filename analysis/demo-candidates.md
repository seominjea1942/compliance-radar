# Demo candidates mined from the real backfill

STATUS: analysis only. Nothing changed in triage, schemas, or UI data. STOP for
human review after reading; demo script and tag set get finalized after this.

Scoring criteria referenced below:
C1 = maps to a specific profile fact / carry-list line. C2 = keyword filter
would plausibly miss it. C3 = has a deadline/effective date/time window.
C4 = concrete and visual. C5 = judge gets it in one sentence.

## 1. Recommended demo beats (from real data)

### Beat A: "It's on your shelf" — Straus Family Creamery ice cream (replaces: generic recalled-deli-product beat)
Real item: STRAUS FAMILY CREAMERY Organic Super Premium Ice Cream, 7 products
(Cookie Dough, Vanilla Bean, Dutch Chocolate, Mint Chip, Strawberry + 2 more),
Class II, initiated 2026-05-12, distribution starts "CA, ...".
One-line why: "Straus Family Creamery is named on your carry list under organic
dairy; seven of their ice cream products were recalled and California is first
on the distribution list." Criteria: C1 (brand named in carry list), C4 (named
flavors), C5. Alternate: Motor City Pizza Co. 5 Cheese Bread (also a literal
carry-list brand, salmonella, nationwide, 2026-05-29) — use whichever shelf
image is easier to stage.

### Beat B: The consent-calendar catch — Lincoln Avenue undergrounding (replaces: dead alcohol/ABC fee beat)
Real item: "Actions Related to the Rule 20A and Rule 20B (In-Lieu Fee)
Underground Utility Program" — CONSENT calendar, 2026-08-11 council meeting.
The title says nothing about any street. Page 2 of the staff report: the
Lincoln Avenue Rule 20A undergrounding project (W San Carlos St to ~160 ft NW
of Coe Ave, plus Auzerais and Paula St segments) slipped one more year;
construction now anticipated June 2029. The in-lieu fee table even prices a
"Lincoln - Lonus to Coe" segment. One-line why: "Buried in a consent item
about a fee program: utility trenching on your own street got rescheduled to
June 2029." Criteria: C2 (this is the strongest keyword-miss in the corpus),
C1 (parking/deliveries fact), C3 (June 2029, delayed from 2028), C4 (mappable
street), C5. This beat carries the "keyword search would miss it" thesis.

### Beat C: Honest silence — the permit map (replaces: forced construction-catch beat)
73 permits within 1500m in 90 days, all residential remodels/ADUs/solar except
a $70k warehouse tenant improvement at 900 Lonus St (997m away, 100 sqft
interior work, already finaled) and a $68k restaurant tenant improvement at
840 Willow St (405m). None affects street parking, deliveries, or foot
traffic; the map shows the store pin plus gray "watched, no impact" pins.
Verdict: do NOT force a catch here. The street-construction threat the
audience expects from permits actually arrived via the council source (Beat B),
which is a better story: sources cover each other's blind spots.

### Beat D: The overturn — Fromm dog food (new; powers the learning-loop beat)
Real item: "Fromm Family Foods Voluntarily Recalls Turkey Pâté Wet Food For
Dogs" — REJECTED with reason "pet food recall unrelated to human food retail."
But Sam's carry list says the store DOES carry pet food (Purina, Blue Buffalo).
Sam overturns with "this actually affects us" and the radar learns pet-food
recalls matter here. Criteria: C1, C5, and it is a genuine triage miss, which
makes the overturn demo honest instead of staged.

Numbers for the home screen (from Task 4): median week = 7 recall items
reviewed, 0 surfaced; 13 of 25 weeks fully silent; peak week 159 items
reviewed, 0 interruptions.

### Task 2: Top 5 council items (from 62 substantive)

1. **Rule 20A/20B Underground Utility Program (m16125, CONSENT, 2026-08-11).**
   What it does: approves the FY25-26 to FY30-31 undergrounding work plan; the
   attachment reschedules Lincoln Avenue construction to June 2029 (one-year
   slip, cascading from the Monterey Road project), scope W San Carlos to near
   Coe Ave plus Auzerais/Paula segments. Touches Sam: multi-month trenching on
   the store's street and delivery route. Consent: YES. Dates: June 2029 start.
2. **Sanitary/Storm Sewer Service Charges public hearing (m16131, regular, 2026-08-11).**
   What it does: confirms FY26-27 sewer charges (~$242M citywide) onto the
   Santa Clara County property tax roll as line "SEWER SANI/STORM"; rates were
   set 2026-06-02 (RES2026-1641/1652). Touches Sam: commercial sewer use
   charges are rate-classed, and food service (the deli) sits in
   higher-strength classes; this lands on the store's tax bill. Consent: no,
   but the title reads like plumbing boilerplate (C2-adjacent). Dates: hearing
   8/11, FY26-27 tax roll.
3. **SJ Cares expansion (m16165, CONSENT, 2026-08-25).** What it does: extends
   San Jose Clean Energy's 10% residential rate discount to ~2,384 more
   affordable-housing units (~$105-125k/yr). Touches Sam: it does NOT: the
   store is a commercial SJCE customer. Value: rejection-log star ("residential
   discount program; the store is a commercial customer"), proving the radar
   reads past a promising title.
4. **City Pay Plan amendment (m16167, CONSENT, 2026-08-25).** What it does:
   creates one city job classification (Council Executive Assistant,
   $79k-$105k) after a union petition. Touches Sam: it does NOT: it is the
   city's own payroll, not private-employer wage law. Value: rejection-log
   star; the naive keyword "pay" would have flagged it (C2 inverted: our
   filter is smarter, in both directions).
5. **Downtown Parking Meter Rates amendment (m16132, regular, 2026-08-11, DEFERRED).**
   What it does: amends the master parking rate schedule for downtown meters;
   deferred at this meeting. Touches Sam: geographically it does not (downtown,
   not Willow Glen), so it is a good filtered item with the parking fact cited;
   the deferral also shows the radar re-watching a moving item.

### Task 5: Surprises (the data knows better)

1. **The Albertsons flood is the best silence story.** 121 of 340 filtered
   recalls are Albertsons store-made items (deli salads, pies, trays) from one
   enforcement wave (week 2026-W19: 159 items in one week). Every reason reads
   "sold exclusively at Albertsons locations." One competitor's bad week would
   have buried a keyword-alert user; the radar filtered all of it with one
   legible sentence. Strong candidate for the rejection-log screen shot.
2. **The Fromm pet-food rejection is a genuine triage miss** (store carries
   pet food) and therefore the most honest possible overturn demo (Beat D).
3. **Six seafood-ish ALERTs contradict the carry list** ("seafood: does not
   carry" yet imitation crab/sashimi/octopus surfaced). The carry-list prompt
   improved packaged-goods reasoning but the model over-weights "family
   markets may stock" for seafood. Fixing this is a one-line prompt nudge;
   alternatively it demos why alert dismissal should also feed learning
   (currently only overturned REJECTIONS do).
4. **The Zapp's/Dirty cascade** (7 SKUs, one seasoning supplier, Class I) is a
   ready-made "one ingredient, many products" narrative if a beat needs depth.
5. **Council items with promising titles rejected on substance** (SJ Cares,
   Pay Plan) are collectively a better "why an LLM and not keywords" argument
   than any single alert: keywords over-alert; the radar reads the memo.
